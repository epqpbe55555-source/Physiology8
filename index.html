The rules, not the lecture

Every governing principle from the chapter, distilled to one line each. No paragraphs — just what you need to recall under pressure.

Filtration vs. Reabsorption

Glomerular filtration is nonselective — everything but plasma proteins gets filtered. Tubular reabsorption is highly selective.

Two Routes

Substances cross the epithelium transcellularly (through the cell) or paracellularly (through tight junctions).

Two Active Mechanisms

Primary active transport is powered directly by ATP hydrolysis (Na⁺-K⁺, H⁺, H⁺-K⁺, Ca²⁺ ATPases). Secondary active transport is powered by the sodium gradient itself.

The Master Pump

The Na⁺-K⁺ ATPase on the basolateral membrane keeps intracellular Na⁺ low, creating the gradient that drives almost everything else.

Final Step = Bulk Flow

From interstitial fluid into the peritubular capillary, movement is by ultrafiltration, governed by hydrostatic and colloid osmotic forces.

Proximal Tubule Design

Loaded with mitochondria and an extensive brush border — built for high-capacity, high-metabolic reabsorption.

Early vs. Late PCT

Early PCT: Na⁺ co-transported with glucose & amino acids. Late PCT: glucose/AA are gone, so Na⁺ reabsorbs mainly with chloride.

The Ascending Limb Rule

Thin and thick ascending limbs are virtually impermeable to water — the anatomical basis of urine concentration.

Two Cells, Late Nephron

Principal cells: reabsorb Na⁺/H₂O, secrete K⁺. Intercalated cells: reabsorb K⁺, secrete H⁺.

ADH Controls the Tap

Late distal tubule & collecting duct water permeability is set entirely by ADH (vasopressin) — none, no water reabsorption.

Medulla-Only Feature

Only the medullary collecting duct is permeable to urea, via dedicated urea transporters.

ANP = Natriuresis

Atrial stretch → ANP → inhibits Na⁺/H₂O reabsorption (mainly collecting ducts) and inhibits renin, so angiotensin II falls too.
